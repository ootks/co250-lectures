Multi-Day Restaurant Planning

When should a restaurant buy ingredients if prices change, storage costs money, and food spoils?

Two dishes share three ingredients

Suppose the restaurant produces chicken bowls and vegetable bowls.

If 10 kg of vegetables remain after day \(t\), only \(0.8(10)=8\) kg are usable on day \(t+1\).

The decisions link production and inventory

\[ \begin{aligned} p_{it}&=\text{kilograms of ingredient \(i\) purchased on day \(t\)},\\ q_{jt}&=\text{servings of dish \(j\) produced on day \(t\)},\\ s_{it}&=\text{usable inventory of ingredient \(i\) after day \(t\)}. \end{aligned} \]

We take the initial inventory to be \(s_{i0}=0\).

Inventory must balance every day

Let \(\alpha_i\) be the fraction of ingredient \(i\) that survives overnight.

\[ \underbrace{\alpha_i s_{i,t-1}}_{\text{inventory that survives}} +\underbrace{p_{it}}_{\text{new purchases}} =\underbrace{\sum_j B_{ij}q_{jt}}_{\text{ingredient used}} +\underbrace{s_{it}}_{\text{inventory stored}}. \]

This is a linear equality in the decision variables.

The objective prices purchasing and storage

Let \(c_{it}\) be the purchase price and \(h_i\) the daily storage cost.

\[ \min_{p,q,s}\quad \sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_i c_{it}p_{it} +\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}\sum_i h_i s_{it}. \]
  • The first term is the total purchase cost.
  • The second term charges for inventory carried to another day.
  • Spoilage appears through the factors \(\alpha_i\) in the balance equations.

The complete model is a linear program

Let \(D_\alpha=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \min_{p,q,s}\quad &\sum_{t=1}^{T}c_t^{\intercal}p_t +\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}h^{\intercal}s_t\\[2mm] \text{such that}\quad &D_\alpha s_{t-1}+p_t=Bq_t+s_t,\qquad &&t=1,\ldots,T,\\ &q_t\ge d_t,\qquad &&t=1,\ldots,T,\\ &p_t,q_t,s_t\ge0,\qquad &&t=1,\ldots,T. \end{aligned} \]

With positive costs, the restaurant produces exactly the required demand.

Demand can instead be a sales limit

If \(d_{jt}\) is the maximum number of dishes that can be sold, impose

\[ 0\le q_{jt}\le d_{jt}. \]

Let \(r_{jt}\) be the revenue from selling dish \(j\) on day \(t\). The restaurant can then maximize profit:

\[ \max_{p,q,s}\quad \sum_{t,j}r_{jt}q_{jt} -\sum_{t,i}c_{it}p_{it} -\sum_{tNow the model decides both what to purchase and which dishes are profitable to produce.
